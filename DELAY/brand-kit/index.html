01 — The idea

What the brand is for

Before colors and fonts, a north star. Every design decision in this guide serves one job: making people feel safe enough to keep reading.

Care that feels like a steady hand — plain-spoken, unhurried, and free of shame — designed so the resource itself lowers a person's guard.

01

Steady, not clinical

Editorial calm over medical sterility. Generous whitespace and warm neutrals signal “you can slow down here.”

02

Warm, not soft-focus

Grounded and credible. The clay accent and confident serif keep it from tipping into precious or overly delicate.

03

For everyone at the table

Neutral by design — a single man, a married couple, or a set of parents should all feel this was made for them.

The signature move: italic + roman

The reference guide gets its editorial feel from mixing Fraunces italic with roman inside one headline — “Teaching Kids about Sex.” Use it on covers and section openers to add lift. Italicize the emotional word, set the rest roman. One mix per headline; never a whole paragraph.

05 — Voice & tone

How it sounds

The words carry as much of the brand as the type. Faith-friendly means values-forward and welcoming — warmth first, scripture optional and never assumed.

Steady
Calm and unhurried. Short, sure sentences. Never anxious or alarmist, even on hard topics.
Plain-spoken
Everyday words over jargon. Say “talk,” not “facilitate a dialogue.” Clarity is kindness.
Shame-free
Curiosity and questions are normal. We name feelings without judging them.
On their side
Cheering the reader on. “You know your people best.” Invitational, never prescriptive.

Say this

  • Let's slow down and take this one step at a time.
  • There's no perfect script — you're already doing the brave thing.
  • You know your family better than anyone.
  • This is normal, and you're not behind.

Not this

  • Failure to address this will damage your child.
  • Experts agree you must follow these seven steps.
  • Utilize evidence-based modalities to facilitate outcomes.
  • If you were a good parent, you'd already know this.
A note before you begin
You don't have to have it all figured out.

This playbook isn't a test. It's a quiet place to notice what you're carrying and set some of it down. Go at your own pace — skip what doesn't fit, sit longer where it does. We'll take it one honest page at a time.

For the two of you
The goal isn't winning. It's understanding.

You're on the same team, even when it doesn't feel like it. These pages give you a shared language and a few gentle prompts — so the hard conversations have somewhere safe to land. Read them together, out loud if you can.

A little reminder for parents
You know your child better than anyone.

Every child is different, and you get to decide what yours is ready for. This guide hands you the words and the timing — you bring the relationship. Trust yourself. We're cheering you on.

07 — Imagery

Photography direction

Photos should feel like the palette: soft natural light, warm neutrals, real and unposed. When in doubt, choose calm over dramatic.

Soft, diffused daylight
Do: window light, airy rooms, breathing space around the subject.
Warm, muted tones
Do: earthy wardrobe, neutral backdrops, gentle contrast — nothing neon.
Real, candid moments
Do: genuine connection and quiet expressions over stiff, staged smiles.
Avoid: harsh & clinical
Don't: cold blue tones, hard flash, stocky corporate posing, busy backgrounds.

08 — Starter kit

Use it anywhere

Drop these tokens into any new playbook, worksheet, or web page and you're on-brand from the first line. Copy and go.

brand-tokens.css
/* ── Warm & grounded · core tokens ── */
:root {
  /* neutrals */
  --ink:        #262019;
  --cream:      #F7F2EA;
  --sand:       #E7DECF;
  --stone:      #A2937E;
  /* accent */
  --clay:       #B4694E;
  --clay-deep:  #9A5238;
  /* type */
  --serif: "Fraunces", Georgia, serif;
  --sans:  "Montserrat", system-ui, sans-serif;
}
Free fonts

Both families are free on Google Fonts. Paste this in your document head:

<head>
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Fraunces:ital,opsz,wght@0,9..144,400..600;1,9..144,400..600&family=Montserrat:wght@300;400;600&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
Applying it to a new playbook
  1. Open on a cover — eyebrow, italic/roman serif headline, tracked-caps subtitle.
  2. One accent per page. Reach for clay only where the eye must land.
  3. Block it in sand for reminders, quotes, and asides.
  4. Close every page with the running foot + serif page number.
