Quick context, so you know whose advice you're holding.

I'm a licensed professional counselor and mental health service provider in Tennessee, and a credentialed minister. I founded a counseling practice that now runs somewhere around ten thousand sessions a year. A lot of those hours have been spent with married couples.

Here's what changed how I work.

Early on I assumed marriages ended because of the big things. An affair. An addiction. Some single catastrophic betrayal you could point at.

That's not what I found. Most of the marriages that came apart in front of me didn't have one big thing. They had a hundred small ones nobody named. A comment let go. A conversation avoided because it was late and everyone was tired. A pattern that got a little more worn every month for six years, until one day two people who genuinely loved each other couldn't figure out how to be in a kitchen together.

That's the good news, oddly. Nobody knows how to undo an affair in five minutes. But a pattern? A pattern you can interrupt. That's most of what I do now, and it's what everything I send you is about.
