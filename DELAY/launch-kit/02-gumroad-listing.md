## Description

> Paste into the description field. Gumroad supports basic formatting. Bold and headings survive.

**"I think I married the wrong person."**

It's the sentence I hear most often in my office, and the one people take the longest to stop
believing.

It's almost never true. In twenty years of counseling, in a practice that now runs around ten thousand sessions a year, the marriages I've watched come apart rarely had one big thing wrong with them.
They had a hundred small ones nobody ever named out loud. A pattern that got a little more worn every
month, until two people who genuinely loved each other couldn't work out how to be in a kitchen
together.

That's the bad news and the good news at once. Nobody undoes a betrayal in five minutes. But a
pattern you can interrupt.

**What's inside · 19 pages**

- Why the wrong-person feeling shows up, and why it's closer to a milestone than a verdict
- The four patterns that account for most recurring fights, described plainly enough that you'll
  recognise yours in a paragraph
- The exact sentences that lower the temperature, actual words rather than "communicate openly"
- A repair sequence for the morning after a fight already went badly
- The small, daily version that makes the big conversations rare

It's written to be worked through by two people in one sitting, not read alone and admired. There are
exercises you hand across the table rather than explain.

**Who it's for**

Couples who are tired, not finished. If there's abuse, active addiction, or a safety concern in your
relationship, a PDF is the wrong tool. Please reach out to a licensed counselor in your state.

**Who wrote it**

Aaron Delay, LPC-MHSP. Licensed professional counselor and mental health service provider, credentialed
minister, founder of a counseling practice in Tennessee. Author of the forthcoming *Saying "I Do"
Everyday*, with a foreword by Gary Chapman.

**A necessary note**

This is an educational resource. It is not psychotherapy, it does not diagnose or treat anything, and
buying it does not create a therapist–client relationship. If you're in crisis, contact a licensed
provider in your state or call or text 988.
