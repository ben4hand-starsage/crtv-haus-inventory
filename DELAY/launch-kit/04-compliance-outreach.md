# The two compliance conversations: start these first

These run on someone else's calendar, which is why section 02 of the strategy puts them in days 1–14
rather than "before launch." Nothing below is legal advice; it's a strategy read on what needs a real
professional to bless it.

Both notes are written for Aaron to send under his own name.

---

## Note 1: To whoever advises the practice on HIPAA

> Send to: the practice's HIPAA/compliance advisor or healthcare attorney

**Subject:** Quick compliance question before I start a marketing email list

Hi [name],

I'm starting a personal-brand email list, separate from the practice, to build an audience for a book
I've written. Free resource in, weekly educational email out, and eventually a paid PDF. None of it
is clinical.

Two things I want you to look at before I do anything:

**1. The client contact list.** I have roughly 3,500 contacts in the practice's system, current and former counseling clients. My understanding is that the fact of someone being my client is protected
health information, and that using that list for marketing would generally require written
authorization. So my plan is not to import any of it. Instead I want clients to be able to opt in
themselves, by their own action, through ordinary practice touchpoints:

- a printed card in the waiting room with a QR code
- an optional line on the intake portal
- a QR code on resource handouts

Can you confirm that approach is sound, and review the exact wording on the card and the portal line
before they go out? I'd rather over-check this than find out later.

**2. Separation.** The marketing list will live in a separate system (Kit) with no connection to the
EHR, and no one will be added to it by me or my staff on a client's behalf. Is there anything else you'd want in place, such as a specific authorization form, a note in the privacy practices, or anything on the
website?

Happy to jump on a call if that's faster.

Thanks,
Aaron

---

## Note 2: To the malpractice carrier and/or a healthcare attorney

> Send to: the malpractice carrier's risk-management line, and separately to a healthcare attorney
> familiar with Tennessee board rules

**Subject:** Structuring a non-clinical coaching offer separately from my counseling practice

Hi [name],

I'm exploring adding a non-clinical marriage coaching program, sold nationally, that would be
deliberately and visibly separate from my counseling practice. Before I build any of it I want to
make sure the structure is right, because I'd rather design it correctly than retrofit it.

What I'm planning:

- A **separate brand, and probably a separate entity**, from the counseling practice
- A **written agreement** stating plainly that coaching is not psychotherapy, involves no diagnosis or
  treatment, and creates no therapist–client relationship
- An **intake screen** that refers anyone presenting with clinical need to appropriate care rather
  than enrolling them
- A **hard rule** that no current or former counseling client is eligible for the coaching program

My questions:

1. Does that structure hold up from a risk perspective, and what would you add to it?
2. Are there Tennessee board rules on advertising or dual relationships I should be reading before I
   describe this publicly?
3. Does my current policy cover, exclude, or care about non-clinical coaching delivered outside
   Tennessee? If it excludes it, what's the right coverage?
4. Is there anything about my licensure that follows me into non-clinical work in other states?

No rush on timing. I'm not selling anything yet, and I'd rather have this settled before I do.

Thanks,
Aaron

## What's already been done on the site

Placeholder disclaimer language is live in the footer of every page and on the opt-in page:

> Everything on this site is educational. It is not psychotherapy, diagnosis, or treatment, and reading
> it does not create a therapist–client relationship. Clinical counseling services are provided
> separately and only to residents of states where Aaron is licensed. If you are in crisis, contact a
> licensed provider in your state or call or text 988.

Treat that as a strategy placeholder, not approved wording. Ask both advisors to review it and replace it with whatever they want it to say. It appears in one place per page and takes two minutes to swap.

The same goes for the "this is educational, not therapy" line on the Gumroad listing and in the
playbook PDF itself.

---

## The one hard rule until both come back

**Do not import the 3,500 client contacts into Kit.** Not as a test, not as a suppression list, not
"just to have them there." A single visible recipient field or forwarded email turns an ordinary
marketing mistake into a licensure problem, and there is no version of the growth plan that needs it. The church channel alone produces more subscribers in one evening than that list is worth as a risk.
