The 5-Minute Script
to stop any marriage argument
before it starts

Fighting is normal. Replaying the same fight until both of you feel misunderstood is exhausting.

The real enemy

The cycle,
not your spouse

One person pushes for an answer. The other pulls away to find space. The more one pushes, the more the other retreats. Then the retreat feels like rejection, so the pressure gets louder.

You are not fighting each other. You are getting caught in a pattern that feeds itself.

Quick check

When tension rises, I usually become the:

Steamroller

I press, explain, repeat, follow, or get louder because I need resolution now.

Turtle

I shut down, go quiet, leave, or avoid because I need safety and space.

Neither role makes you the villain. Both reactions make sense. Left unchecked, they keep the argument alive.

The Circuit
Breaker

Five minutes to stop the spiral and protect the connection.

1

Name the pattern

“We are in the Steamroller-Turtle cycle right now. I do not want to fight you. I want us to slow this down.”

2

Say what is underneath

“I am feeling because . What I need right now is .”

3

Make one clear request

“Would you be willing to ?”

Keep it specific, small, and possible tonight.

Replace blame with clarity
Instead of

“You always ignore me and never help.”

Try

“I am feeling overwhelmed right now. I need support with . Can you help by ?”

The time-out rule

“I care about this, and I am too activated to talk well. I need 20 minutes. I will come back at : so we can finish this together.”

A pause without a return time feels like abandonment. Set the time, then honor it.

Your five-minute reset

Write it before
you say it

A calmer sentence is easier to find on paper than in the middle of a fight.

What happened, without always or never?
What am I feeling beneath the anger?
What do I need, in one clear sentence?
What small action am I asking for?
If we pause, what exact time will we return?
