The approach

Plain words for the hardest conversations you'll ever have.

I'm not interested in making you feel studied. Three things shape everything I write.

Steady, not clinical

Nothing here diagnoses you. The goal is a lower heart rate and a clearer next sentence, not a label.

Shame-free by design

Couples arrive convinced they're the broken exception. They almost never are. Most of what you're doing is ordinary, and ordinary is fixable.

Faith-friendly, never assumed

Written for anyone with a marriage. I'm a credentialed minister, so my values show up in how I write. Nothing here asks you to share them, and nothing stops working if you don't.

A note before you start

You didn't marry the wrong person.

That's the sentence I say most often, and it's the one people take longest to believe. When a marriage gets hard, it's easy to wonder if you chose wrong. But so often, what feels like incompatibility is actually a handful of moves neither of you was ever taught.

Aaron and his wife. He gets to practise what he writes about, which keeps him honest.

Who's writing this

I do this work because I need it too.

I'm a licensed professional counselor and mental health service provider in Tennessee, a credentialed minister, and the founder of a practice that sees around ten thousand sessions a year. I've sat with a lot of marriages at their worst hour.

I'm also married, with two kids and the same tired Tuesday evenings as everyone else. None of what I write here is theory I admire from a distance. It's what my wife and I actually use, and what I keep watching work in the room.

Aaron
