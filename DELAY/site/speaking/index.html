The evening

What actually happens

No altar call, no product pitch from the stage, no sales table at the back. One useful evening that reflects well on the church that hosted it.

  1. Twenty minutes: why marriages drift The pattern underneath most of it, named out loud. This is the part where the room relaxes, because everybody realises they're normal.
  2. Thirty minutes: the four repairs The specific moves that change how a hard conversation goes. Concrete enough to use in the car on the way home.
  3. Twenty-five minutes: couples work the worksheet Together, at their own table. Quiet, unglamorous, and the part people tell me about afterwards.
  4. Fifteen minutes: questions Anonymous cards or open floor, whichever your room prefers.

Questions pastors ask

The practical bits

Will you recruit our people as counseling clients?

No. I don't take clients from the room, and I won't sell anything from the stage. If someone asks for a referral I'll point them to a provider near them.

How much notice do you need?

Six to eight weeks is comfortable, mostly so your team has time to promote it properly. A poorly promoted night is the only way this goes wrong.

How far will you travel?

Anywhere within a few hours of Memphis without discussion. Further afield, let's talk. Travel costs are the only thing I'd ever ask a church to cover.

How big does the group need to be?

It works from about twenty couples up. Smaller than that and it's better as a conversation than a talk, which I'm also happy to do.

Is it denominationally specific?

No. I'm a credentialed minister and the values are visible, but the content is clinical work in plain language and travels comfortably across traditions.
