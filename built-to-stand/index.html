THE CAST

Who's on stage

YOUBuilder 1. Takes the job seriously. Reads the blueprint. Works hard. Not boring or uptight — just convinced the instructions matter.
HERBuilder 2. Confident, funny, shortcuts everything. She isn't "bad." She genuinely thinks she's figured out an easier way to do life.
Character note: She's not trying to destroy her house. She wants the same result without following the instructions. Play the confidence, not the villainy.
BOSSAppears or calls in once (Scene 3). The one who gave the blueprint. Warm, not scary.
0:00 – 2:00SCENE 1

The Job

≈ 2 min
Stage Picture — pre-set before doors
  • Two house-building areas
  • Building materials
  • Large pile / container of sand
  • Rocks or simulated bedrock
  • Two houses / structures
  • Blueprints (x2)
  • Construction tools
  • Lawn chair, drink, Nintendo Switch
  • Bible — hidden but accessible for later
🔊
SoundConstruction ambience runs underneath the whole scene — hammers, distant saw. Keep it low under dialogue.
Story Beat
You both enter as construction workers ready for a huge assignment.
Establish that the Boss hired you to build houses and gave you blueprints. The blueprint specifically calls for a strong foundation.
Her Choice
She discovers the sand. It's lighter. It's easier. It's right there. The rocks are heavy and inconvenient. She immediately chooses sand.
🧰
PropsHER makes a big physical show of how heavy the rocks are vs. how easy the sand pours. Sell the contrast.
Key Interaction
YOUThe blueprint says we need a strong foundation.
HERThose things are basically suggestions.
HER can riff: the blueprints are old, outdated, complicated, who-even-reads-these, etc.
YOUThe person we're building this for gave us the instructions. I think we should probably listen to him.
She disagrees.
🌱 Underlying Lesson — don't teach yet

Just let the kids see the first mistake: she wants to build the house her way instead of the Builder's way. Plant it. Don't explain it.

2:00 – 5:00SCENE 2

Easy vs. Right

≈ 3 min
The contrast between the two builders gets bigger.
Her Side
She gets her house up ridiculously fast. She celebrates. She's proud of herself. Meanwhile you're still carrying rocks, checking measurements, working.
She starts mocking how unnecessarily difficult you're making everything. Then comes the lawn chair. Drink. Feet up. Maybe sunglasses. She's living the dream.
🧰
PropsLawn chair, drink, sunglasses staged within reach for HER. Switch stays hidden until the beat below.
Her Philosophy
Important character beat — she isn't simply "lazy." It's: "Why do something the hard way when there's an easier way?"
HERLook at me. House built. Relaxing. Look at you. Sweating. Who's the smart builder here?
Video Game Beat
🧰
PropsHER pulls out the Nintendo Switch and invites YOU to play.
YOUThere's nothing wrong with playing video games. But right now, I've got a job to finish.
Then introduce the coming storm — you've checked the forecast, bad weather is coming.
🎬
ScreenOptional: flash a cartoon weather forecast graphic on the video wall as YOU "checks" it.
HERThe weather guy is wrong all the time.
🌱 Core Idea being illustrated — still planting

Discipline is choosing what you need to do over what you feel like doing.

Don't fully teach it yet. You're planting it.

5:00 – 6:30SCENE 3

The Boss Checks In

≈ 1.5 min
This is the honesty setup.
🔊
SoundIf BOSS calls in rather than enters — phone ring SFX / walkie crackle to cue the moment.
The Boss enters or calls. He asks how everything is going. You report honestly. Then he asks her.
She's been sitting around playing video games. She immediately jumps up and pretends she's exhausted from working.
Her Lie
HEROh, it's going GREAT. I've been working SO hard. Haven't stopped all day.
You just stare at her. She stares back. Maybe the kids react. Don't correct her yet — let the lie hang there.
🌱 Why this beat matters

The lie comes because telling the truth would cost her something. That's exactly what you return to later.

Honesty means telling the truth even when a lie would make things easier.

6:30 – 8:00SCENE 4

The Nap

≈ 1.5 min
The Boss leaves. She celebrates getting away with it. Then she's tired. Time for a nap. She gets comfortable and falls asleep. You're still working.
This is your strongest audience interaction before the storm.
Audience Interaction
👥
AudienceStop working. Show you're exhausted. Look at her → look at your unfinished house → look at the kids.
YOUGuys, I'm tired. Should I just quit and take a nap too?
Let them yell. Push back a little:
YOUBut look at her! She's having a great time!
Let them convince you. Then:
YOUAll right. You're right. I've got a job to finish.
Back to work.
This quietly gets the kids to choose discipline themselves — before you've taught the word.

Don't rush this moment. It's the visual payoff of the entire message.

10:00 – 13:00SCENE 6

Lunch at the Ruins

≈ 3 min · the turn
Her energy changes. Not melodramatic — she's genuinely confused.
HERMy house is gone.
She starts frantically trying to rebuild. You stop her — it's lunchtime. You both sit. Her destroyed house sits right there while you talk. That's the perfect backdrop.
🧰
SetKeep the collapsed house in full view during the whole conversation — do not clear it.
Her Realization — she connects the dots herself
HERI probably should've used those rocks.
YOUYep.
HERAnd I probably shouldn't have played video games when I was supposed to be working.
YOUProbably.
HERAnd the nap?
YOUDefinitely the nap.
HERHow did you know?
That's the doorway.
🔑 The Bible Reveal
🧰
Props — key momentYOU pull out the Bible that was hidden in Scene 1.
HERNo, no. I asked how you knew how to build the house.
YOUI know. This is where I learned it.
That should feel surprising.
Matthew 7 Reveal
Read or paraphrase Matthew 7:24–27. Jesus describes two people. Both build. Both experience rain, floods, wind. One house stands. One falls.
📖 Land this

Jesus wasn't really teaching us how to build houses. He was teaching us how to build lives.

The difference wasn't the storm — both houses got hit. The difference was what they were built on.

Jesus said the wise person hears what He says and does it. That's what it means to build on the Rock.

13:00 – 17:00SCENE 7

Three Ways to Build Strong

≈ 4 min · the teaching
Now use the entire skit as your sermon illustration. You don't need three mini-sermons — point back to what the kids already watched.
1 · Honesty — point back to the Boss scene
HEROkay, so what does building on the Rock actually look like?
YOURemember when the Boss asked if you had been working?
She remembers.
YOUWhat did you tell him?
She admits she lied.
💡 Big Idea — Honesty

Building a strong life means telling the truth even when lying would be easier.

Because Jesus teaches us to live truthfully. Sometimes telling the truth means admitting:

  • "I did it." · "I broke it."
  • "I didn't finish my homework."
  • "I wasn't where I said I was."
  • "I treated my brother or sister badly."

Every time you choose truth, you're putting another piece of your life on a strong foundation.

HERSo every time I tell the truth, even when it's hard, I'm building on the Rock?
YOUExactly.
2 · Discipline — point back to the Switch & the nap
💡 Big Idea — Discipline

Discipline means doing what you're supposed to do even when something easier or more fun is available.

Make it concrete. Sometimes building a strong life looks like:

  • Doing homework when you'd rather play.
  • Cleaning your room when you'd rather watch TV.
  • Practicing when you'd rather quit.
  • Obeying your parents the first time.
  • Finishing something you started.
  • Doing your best even when nobody is watching.

There's nothing wrong with resting or playing. The problem is when what I want to do keeps me from doing what I need to do.

👥
AudienceAsk: "Kids Life, what should she have done when it was time to work — play video games or finish the job?" Let them answer.
HEROkay, okay. I get it.
3 · Follow the Blueprint — pick the blueprint back up
🧰
PropsYOU physically pick the blueprint back up. This one is the biggest of the three.
💡 Big Idea — God's Word

God's Word is the blueprint that teaches us how to build our lives.

The blueprint told you how to build the house — you didn't have to guess. In the same way, God hasn't left us guessing about how to live. The Bible teaches us:

  • How to treat people · how to forgive · how to obey
  • How to handle anger · how to be a good friend
  • How to work · how to love · how to tell the truth
  • How to follow Jesus
▲ Important Theological Turn — go one step deeper
🧰
PropsOpen to John 1. Don't leave the Rock as merely "the Bible."
📖 John 1:1, 14 — the Word became flesh

The Bible isn't giving us random rules for becoming better people. It's teaching us to know Jesus, hear Jesus, and follow Jesus.

A strong life is built by hearing what Jesus says and doing it.

Call & Response — have the kids repeat
YOU: "Kids Life, say this with me…"
HEAR WHAT JESUS SAYS…
↳ kids repeat
…AND DO IT!
↳ kids repeat · then again, LOUDER:
HEAR WHAT JESUS SAYS AND DO IT!
17:00 – 19:30SCENE 8

Fresh Start

≈ 2.5 min · salvation
She looks at her destroyed house. Then the blueprint. Then the Bible.
HERI built this house wrong. But what if I've been building my life wrong too?
This is your natural transition into salvation — no need to stop the play to tack on an altar call.
YOUYou can start again.
✝ Salvation Bridge

Jesus doesn't just want to make you a nicer person or a harder worker. Jesus came to save us from our sin.

He died for us. He rose again. And we can choose to trust Him, follow Him, and build our lives on Him.

She asks if she can do that. Absolutely. Then, before praying:
YOUBut I don't think you're the only one who might want to make that decision.
👥
Audience — Fresh Start MomentTurn toward Kids Life. Some may realize: "I've never decided that Jesus is going to be the foundation of my life." Invite them to make a Fresh Start with Jesus. Give the normal Kids Life response opportunity, then pray together.
19:30 – 20:00SCENE 9

Let's Build Again

≈ 0.5 min · button
After prayer, turn back toward her. Look at the destroyed house. She picks up the blueprint. Then she picks up a rock.
HERSo… where do we start?
YOUWith the foundation.
Both start rebuilding.
🎬
Screen — final statementPush the closing statement to the video wall as the lights come up.
Final screen statement
HEAR WHAT JESUS SAYS.
DO WHAT HE SAYS.
BUILD A LIFE THAT STANDS.
🎯 The message in one sentence — if everything goes sideways
A strong life is built by hearing what Jesus says and doing it.

If time gets cut, improv runs long, or you forget something — this is the sentence you cannot lose. Honesty, discipline, and God's Word all live underneath it. The goal isn't "Christians work hard and don't lie." The goal is: "Jesus knows how to build my life. I can listen to Him, trust Him, and do what He says." That keeps the message centered on Jesus — not on character development.
